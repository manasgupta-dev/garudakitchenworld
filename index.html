The steel

304 or 202 — know which one you're carrying home

Both are honest products. They cost different money because they are different metal, and across a busy counter the difference is not always visible. So here it is in writing.

Comparison of 304 and 202 grade stainless steel
Property304 grade202 grade
Also called 18/8 food grade 200 series
Nickel content Around 8% — the nickel is what resists rust Around 1–4%, with manganese in its place
Rust resistance Highest. Handles salt, tamarind and long soaking Good in dry use. Can spot if left wet or with salt
Best for Cooking vessels, hotel service, anything acidic or stored wet Dry storage, serving, decorative and gifting pieces
Price Higher — you are paying for the nickel Lower, at the same look and finish
At our counter Stated on request, every item Stated on request, every item

Ask for the grade on anything. If we can't tell you, don't buy it.

Buying from us

What you're paying for

Steel is sold by grade and by weight. Here is what we commit to at the counter.

Grade is stated

Ask for the grade on any item and we'll tell you — 304 and 202 are both honest products at different prices, but you should know which one you're carrying home.

Weight is checked

Where an item is priced by weight, we'll weigh the piece you're buying in front of you, not a sample of it.

Wholesale rates

Quantity pricing for hotels, canteens, caterers and resellers. Bring your requirement list and we'll price the whole sheet.

Sizes arranged

If a size or quantity isn't on the shelf, most of it can be brought in within a few days. Ask before you settle for the nearest thing.
